## The arc of the day

**Morning: shared practice case, in pairs.** Everyone works on the same
fictional business ([labs/practice-case.md](../labs/practice-case.md)), so the
debriefs compare *prompts*, not ideas. When every pair has the same input,
differences in output come from the prompting, and the room can see that.

**Afternoon: your own idea, individually.** Participants switch to the idea
they named in Module 00. A partner stays alongside as tester and critic. This is
what makes the day useful on Monday.

## Why this shape

- **Orientation is short.** Twenty minutes is enough to set expectations and
  fix logins; longer and the room loses energy before any content.
- **The prompt brief sits at 10:35**, when attention is highest. It's the skill
  everything after lunch depends on.
- **The post-lunch block is the most hands-on** (80% activity), and it's the
  block on the participant's own idea, which is the most motivating material of
  the day.
- **The capstone produces something to take home**: a tested template, not
  just notes.
- **Two breaks, roughly every 75–90 minutes of work.** Don't trade them for
  content time.
