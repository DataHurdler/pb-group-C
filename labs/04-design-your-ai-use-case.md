# Lab 04 — Capstone: Design Your AI Use Case

**Time:** 55 minutes · **Work in:** individually, then swap with your partner
(step 8)
**Module:** 04 · **Objective:** LO4

## What you'll do

Pick one task you do again and again, work out where AI fits in it, build and
test a prompt for that part, and fill in a one-page **use-case canvas** you
could show a manager, a co-founder, or yourself: what it saves, what could go
wrong, and how you'll pilot it.

The one-page version of this task is in
[handouts/capstone-brief.md](../handouts/capstone-brief.md).

## What you need

- Your AI assistant
- Your prompt log from Labs 02–03
- [The use-case canvas](../handouts/use-case-canvas.md)
- [The template format](../handouts/reference.md#template-format)

## Steps

1. **Pick a recurring task (5 min).** Something you do **at least monthly**
   that takes **30 minutes or more**. For example:
   - A weekly report or update to your team, investors, or clients
   - Answering a common type of customer question
   - Turning meeting or interview notes into actions and themes
   - Drafting proposals, job ads, or product descriptions
   - Reviewing monthly figures and writing a summary

2. **Map it (5 min).** Write the 3–6 steps you follow today. Mark each step:
   **AI does** · **AI helps, I decide** · **Me only**. Pick the step where AI
   would save the most time.

3. **Criteria first (5 min).** Before running anything, write 3–4 yes/no checks
   a good output must pass. For example: *"Under 300 words." "Every number
   matches the source." "Nothing I couldn't say to the client directly." "I'd
   send it with only small edits."*

4. **Build, run, score (10 min).** Write the prompt using the full brief. Run
   it on a realistic, non-confidential example. Score each criterion pass or
   fail.

5. **Change one thing at a time (10 min).** Take a failed criterion, change
   **one** part of the prompt, rerun, rescore. Log it: *what I changed → what
   happened.* Aim for two rounds.

6. **Templatize (5 min).** Replace the specifics with `[SLOTS]` and add the
   header: **Use this for** / **Don't use this for** / **Check before using the
   output**.

7. **Fill in the canvas (8 min).** Complete the rest of the
   [use-case canvas](../handouts/use-case-canvas.md): time saved, data and
   policy, risks and checks, and a two-week pilot.

8. **Partner test (7 min).** Swap. Your partner runs your template with their
   own details in the slots, reads your canvas, and answers: *"Would I approve
   this pilot? What's the one question I'd ask first?"* Fix what they flag.

## You're done when

You have a filled-in canvas with a tested template attached, a log of at least
one one-change round, and a partner's answer to "would I approve this?"

## If you finish early

Add a worked example to your template (an excellent output, under "Here is an
example of the quality and format I want") and rerun. Did the output become
more consistent?

## Debrief questions

1. How many hours a month would your use case save, honestly?
2. What's the biggest risk on your canvas, and what's the check for it?

### Instructor notes (remove from the participant copy)

- **Expected output.** A canvas with a template of about 8–15 lines and 2–5
  slots. Expect optimistic time-saved estimates: in one controlled study,
  developers expected a 24% speed-up and were measured 19% slower ([METR, 2025, *Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity*](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)).
  Ask "Does that include the time to check the output?"
- **Where people get stuck.** (a) Vague criteria: push for yes/no in step 3,
  it's the hinge of the lab. (b) Changing several things per round: ask
  "What did you change?" and wait. (c) A one-off task: ask "When will you do
  this again?" (d) The data box. Owners can decide their own policy; employees
  may not know theirs. "Find out" is a valid pilot step.
- **Post canvases** to the shared document at the end of step 8.
- **At 15:25,** call time and ask each pair to choose one canvas for a
  60-second pitch in the wrap-up.
