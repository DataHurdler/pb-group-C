# Lab 04 — Capstone: Build Your Prompt Kit

**Time:** 55 minutes · **Work in:** individually, then swap with your partner
(step 6)
**Module:** 04 · **Objective:** LO4

## What you'll do

Turn one prompt into a tool you'll use again. Decide what "good" means before
you run it, improve it one change at a time, and package it so someone else can
use it. Your partner tests it on their own idea.

The one-page version of this task is in
[handouts/capstone-brief.md](../handouts/capstone-brief.md).

## What you need

- Your AI assistant
- Your prompt log from Labs 02–03
- [The template format](../handouts/reference.md#template-format)

## Steps

1. **Pick a recurring task (5 min).** Choose something you'll do **more than
   once** on your idea or in your work. Examples:
   - Turn customer interview notes into themes and next questions
   - Draft a one-page pitch for a specific audience
   - Write a weekly update for partners or investors
   - Scan a market and summarize what's changed
   - Pressure-test any new idea (your Lab 03 chain)

2. **Write your criteria first (5 min).** Before running anything, write 3–4
   checks a good output must pass. Make them yes/no, for example: *"Under 300
   words." "Names the customer segment." "Every number has a source or is
   flagged as an estimate." "I'd send it with only small edits."*

3. **Run and score (10 min).** Write the prompt using the full brief. Run it.
   Score the output against each criterion: pass or fail.

4. **Change one thing at a time (15 min).** Pick the criterion that failed.
   Change **one** part of the prompt to fix it, rerun, rescore. Log each round:
   *what I changed → what happened.* Aim for at least two rounds.

5. **Templatize (10 min).** Replace the specifics with `[SLOTS]` in capitals,
   such as `[MY CUSTOMER]` and `[NOTES PASTED HERE]`. Add a header to the template:
   - **Use this for:** one line
   - **Don't use this for:** one line on where you wouldn't trust it
   - **Check before using the output:** your criteria from step 2

6. **Partner test (10 min).** Swap templates. Your partner fills in the slots
   for *their* idea and runs it, then tells you: was it clear what to put in each
   slot? Did the output pass your criteria? Fix the one thing that confused them.

## You're done when

You have a template with slots, a use / don't-use header, your criteria, and a
log of at least two one-change rounds, and someone other than you has run it
successfully.

## If you finish early

Add a worked example: an excellent output pasted into the template under
"Here is an example of the quality and format I want." Rerun with your
partner's slots. Did consistency improve?

## Debrief questions

1. Which single change made the biggest difference?
2. What did you write under "Don't use this for", and why?

---

### Instructor notes (remove from the participant copy)

- **Expected output.** A template of roughly 8–15 lines with 2–5 slots. A
  common weakness is criteria that are vibes ("is good", "is professional").
  Push for yes/no checks in step 2. It's the hinge of the lab.
- **Where people get stuck.** (a) Changing several things at once in step 4,
  then not knowing what helped. Ask "what did you change?" and wait. (b)
  Choosing a one-off task in step 1. Ask "When will you do this again?"
- **Post templates** to the shared document (open-questions Q16) at the end of
  step 6, so the room leaves with everyone's kit.
- **At 15:25** call time and ask each pair to choose one template and one
  before/after to show in the wrap-up.
