# Practice Case — Loop Laundry

*A fictional business for practice. Every name and number below is invented.
Use it for Labs 01 and 02, so everyone in the room works from the same facts.*

---

**The business.** Loop Laundry is a self-service laundromat in a mid-sized
city, opened six years ago by **Dana Reyes**. It has 24 washers and 18 dryers,
two part-time staff, and a small drop-off wash-and-fold service that's grown
steadily.

**The numbers (last 12 months, invented).**

- Revenue: $310,000. About 80% self-service, 20% wash-and-fold
- Wash-and-fold: about 140 orders a week, average $28 per order
- Busiest hours: weekday evenings and weekend mornings; weekday 10:00–15:00 is
  quiet
- Two new apartment complexes (about 600 units in total) opened within a mile
  in the past year. Most units have no in-unit laundry

**The idea Dana is considering.** A **pickup-and-delivery laundry
subscription** for busy professionals and families: a monthly plan, collected
from your door and returned folded within 48 hours.

**Dana's constraints.**

- Up to **$15,000** to invest. No outside investors
- Can't hire a full-time employee in the first six months
- Doesn't own a vehicle suitable for deliveries
- Wants a decision within **three months**
- Worries about cannibalizing the existing wash-and-fold customers

**What Dana doesn't know.** Whether nearby residents would pay for a
subscription, at what price, how competitors (if any) price similar services,
and whether delivery logistics can work without a full-time driver.

---

*For instructors: the case is deliberately incomplete. There are no competitor
names, local prices, or market-size figures, so a model that "provides" them is
inventing them. That's the point of Lab 01, step 4.*
